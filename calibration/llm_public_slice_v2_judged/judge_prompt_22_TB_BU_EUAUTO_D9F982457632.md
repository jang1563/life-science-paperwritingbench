Abstract
DNA polymerase theta (POLQ) is implicated in tumor progression, but its role in colorectal cancer (CRC) is not fully defined. Bioinformatics analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas data revealed POLQ was overexpressed in 21 malignancies, including colon adenocarcinoma (COAD). In 48 paired clinical specimens, immunohistochemistry and western blotting confirmed POLQ was significantly upregulated in CRC tissues compared to para-carcinoma tissues (P<0.01 and P=0.01, respectively). High POLQ expression correlated with poor overall survival in these patients (P=0.033). Functional enrichment analysis of 251 POLQ co-expressed genes indicated involvement in cell cycle pathways. Experimentally, stable knockdown of POLQ in SW480 and HCT116 CRC cells significantly reduced proliferation, migration, and invasion. This was associated with decreased expression of MMP2 and MMP9 and inhibition of G1/M and S/M phase cell cycle proteins. Bioinformatic assessment of the tumor immune microenvironment linked high POLQ expression to altered infiltration of memory B cells, follicular helper T cells, and resting NK cells, and to differential expression of immune checkpoints including CD274. Drug sensitivity analysis suggested high POLQ expression was associated with lower IC50 for lapatinib and crizotinib but higher IC50 for roscovitine and salubrinal. These findings indicate POLQ promotes CRC aggressiveness and may influence the immune landscape and therapeutic response.
