## Abstract
Melasma is a recurrent and treatment-resistant hyperpigmentation disorder characterized by a complex and multifactorial pathogenesis. However, the lack of a stable and reliable animal model has hindered systematic investigations into its onset and progression. In this study, we established a melasma-like model in C57BL/6J mice by combining broadband UVB irradiation, intramuscular progesterone administration, and induced emotional stress. The affected skin areas exhibited irregular, brown hyperpigmented patches. Histopathological analysis revealed an accumulation of melanin granules in the epidermis and superficial dermis, elevated levels of tyrosinase (TYR) in both skin and plasma, systemic oxidative stress imbalance, and reduced autophagic activity in the lesional skin. Furthermore, this model displayed distinct differences from a UV-induced post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) model. Notably, the melasma-like mice responded to tranexamic acid treatment in a manner that closely resembled clinical outcomes observed in human patients. Collectively, these findings establish a stable, reproducible, and clinically relevant mouse model of melasma, providing a valuable platform for future research into its pathogenesis and treatment.

3. Results 3.1. Establishment and Validation of a Melasma Mouse Model 3.1.1. Skin Appearance and Pigmentation Assessment in the Melasma Mouse Model We selected 6‐8‐week‐old female C57BL/6J mice and induced melasma over a 28‐day period using a combination of broad‐spectrum UVB, progesterone and restraint (Figure 1A ). Macroscopic observations showed that, compared to controls, the melasma model mice (Mel1 and Mel2 groups) displayed mild erythema and desquamation on the head by day 7, without visible pigmentation (Figure 2A ). By day 14, speckled pigmentation began to emerge, accompanied by slight desquamation. The pigmentation progressively darkened, merging into brown patches, and by day 28, the skin exhibited distinct irregular brown patches with central speckling, which closely resembled clinical melasma lesions. In the ear model group (Figure 2B ), similar changes were observed. By day 7, the left UVB‐exposed ear in the model group showed mild wrinkling, desquamation and capillary dilation, with no visible changes in the right ear. Over time, the desquamation and vascular dilation resolved, and pigmentation gradually intensified in the affected areas. Overall skin observations suggest that C57BL/6J mice constitute an effective model for studying pigmented disorders such as melasma. Compared to ear modelling, head modelling yields a skin phenotype that more closely resembles clinical melasma. ## Candidate output (to be judged)

Methods

A melasma-like mouse model was established using 6-8-week-old female C57BL/6J mice subjected to a 28-day protocol combining broadband UVB irradiation, intramuscular progesterone, and emotional stress (Fig. 1A). Two modelling sites were used: the shaved head and the left ear. For the head group, UVB was delivered at 30 mJ/cm², while the left ear received 60 mJ/cm²; the right ear was shielded with tinfoil, and both eyes were protected (Fig. 1A). Progesterone was injected intramuscularly daily at two doses: a low-dose (P1: 0.4 mg/kg) and a high-dose (P2: 4 mg/kg), with injections alternated between hindlimbs (Fig. 1A). Emotional stress was induced using a rotating schedule of three restraint methods applied daily: 24-hour fasting or water deprivation (used twice weekly, never consecutively), confinement in a restraint device for 5 minutes, and tail-overhanging handstand for 2 minutes (Fig. 1A). This combined protocol was performed daily for 28 days, with photographs and observations recorded every 7 days.

Pigmentation changes in the modelling areas were assessed macroscopically and quantified by analyzing grayscale values from photographs using ImageJ software (Fig. 2A, B, C). For comparison, a separate UV-induced hyperpigmentation model was created using the same UVB dose but without progesterone or stress factors, with daily exposure for 28 days (Fig. 4A). In a therapeutic intervention, a subset of melasma-like mice received a daily oral gavage of tranexamic acid at 65 mg/kg for the 28-day modelling period (Fig. 6A).

Following the modelling period, mice were euthanized for tissue and serum collection (Fig. 1A). Skin tissues from modelled and control areas were processed for histopathological analysis using Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) and Masson-Fontana melanin staining (Fig. 3A, Fig. 5A). Melanin deposition was quantified by calculating the ratio of melanin granules to total epidermal and dermal area, epidermal area alone, and dermal area alone (Fig. 3B, C, D). Levels of tyrosinase (TYR), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and malondialdehyde (MDA) were assessed in skin and plasma, though the specific assays are not detailed in the provided evidence. Autophagy activity in lesional skin was evaluated via immunofluorescence staining for LC3 and p62 proteins, with relative fluorescence intensities quantified using ImageJ software (Fig. 8A, B, C, D). Specific sample sizes for each experimental group and the statistical tests used for comparisons (indicated by p-values in figures) are not provided in the evidence.
